Data. NASA C-MAPSS simulated turbofan engines run to failure (FD001–FD004). LightGBM predicts remaining life; every engine is predicted by a model that never saw it (5-fold engine cross-fitting), repeated for 10 random seeds. The engines are simulated, so the euros illustrate the method and are not a forecast for any airline.

Maintenance model. The first alert books a removal after the notice period. If the engine is still alive it is a planned removal and its remaining life is wasted; otherwise it fails. Cost per cycle flown = (failures × failure cost + planned removals × planned cost) ÷ cycles flown, so throwing life away is never free.

Two views. The recommendation and the first table use dials tuned on separate calibration engines and applied once to unseen engines, as in the thesis; they are computed at the tested cost ratios (1, 1.5, 2, 3, 5, 10, 20, 50, 100) and the nearest one is shown. The dial explorer picks settings with hindsight for any ratio, an upper bound on what tuning can do.

Dials. Safety margin: alert when the predicted life is within notice + margin. Confidence level: alert when the lower end of the prediction interval reaches the notice period (higher level = earlier alert). Age: replace every engine at a fixed age, no sensors.

Freedom. In the thesis results a riskier confidence level costs far more than a more cautious one (one step riskier: +1 to +29 %; one step more cautious: +0.2 to +4 %). "Caution only" lets the planner move the alert earlier but never later than the recommendation.
